How your material is handled

This statement describes, in plain terms, where your documents live, which systems touch them, what leaves our environment and what does not. Every clause below describes a control that is in place today, and Section 8 explains how you can verify it yourself.

1. Scope

It applies to all material received from {{CLIENT}} in connection with the engagement: tender and RFP documentation, bills of quantities, pricing schedules, drawings and specifications, correspondence, credentials, and any derived working files we create from them.

Enforced, not promised. The processing runtime is technically prevented from reaching external endpoints: the client library only accepts a loopback address, and every processing run executes inside a socket-level egress jail that blocks outbound connections and hostname lookups. Blocked attempts are recorded in an audit log. Section 8 shows the one-command demonstration.

3. Boundaries we do not hide

  • Correspondence platforms. E-mail and messaging are delivered through third-party providers and stored on their systems under their own terms. We recommend specific document transfer methods where confidentiality is critical.
  • Generic assistance. Non-confidential general queries may be handled with commercial cloud AI services. Your documents, client names, prices and correspondence never form part of such queries, and the sensitivity gate described in Section 8 is what prevents it.
  • Hosting. Our instance runs in a dedicated cloud account in a single region, in our name and under our control. Hosting disk encryption is not part of our claims: confidentiality of documents rests on the per-client encryption described in Section 4.

4. Encryption

  • Per-client archives: AES-256 (OpenPGP symmetric, iterated SHA-512 key derivation), one passphrase per client. A key for one client cannot open another client's archive.
  • Integrity: every archive is recorded in a manifest with its SHA-256 hash; verification detects any modification of the stored file.
  • Backups: encrypted on the processing machine before transfer; the storage provider holds ciphertext only. Local copies are retained for rapid restoration and are rotated.
  • Key custody: passphrases are held in a 0600 permission key file and in our password manager. A copy is provided to you on request so that you are never dependent on us.

5. Access control

  • Access is limited to the named individuals assigned to the engagement; there are no shared accounts.
  • Your archive passphrase is unique, so access can be revoked per client by rotating that key alone.
  • Documents are not copied to personal devices, consumer cloud drives, or third-party web tools.

7. Retention, deletion and incidents

  • Retention: working material is kept for {{RETENTION_DAYS}} days after the final submission unless a different period is agreed in writing; vault archives hold {{VAULT_ARCHIVES}} archive(s) for this engagement at the time of issue.
  • Deletion: on written request, archives, working copies and indexes are deleted within 5 business days, with a deletion confirmation that names the archives removed.
  • Incident notice: we notify {{CLIENT}} without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours of becoming aware of any incident affecting the confidentiality or integrity of the material.

8. Verification

  1. Encryption: run zeroegress vault verify --client <your-name> - decrypts each archive, checks the SHA-256 against the manifest, and lists the members. It prints nothing readable unless a passphrase is supplied.
  2. Local-only processing: run zeroegress prove - starts the egress jail, attempts three external endpoints, and produces a local model answer while those attempts are blocked. The blocked attempts are printed.
  3. Sensitivity gate: run zeroegress gate <file> - shows which signals in a document classify it as local-only before anything is sent anywhere.
  4. No provider keys: run zeroegress status - reports the model endpoint and lists any third-party provider secrets present in the environment.

9. Confidentiality terms ready for the engagement agreement

  1. Confidential Information means all information disclosed by {{CLIENT}} in connection with the engagement, in any form, including tender documentation, pricing, technical specifications, drawings, correspondence and any derived material.
  2. Permitted purpose. The provider shall use Confidential Information solely to perform the engagement and for no other purpose, including model training or product development.
  3. No third-party AI processing. The provider shall not submit Confidential Information to any third-party artificial-intelligence service. Automated analysis shall be performed only on self-hosted models within the provider's controlled environment.
  4. Security measures. The provider shall maintain the controls set out in Sections 2 to 6, shall not weaken them during the term, and shall notify {{CLIENT}} before any material change.
  5. Disclosure. Disclosure to employees and sub-processors is permitted only on a need-to-know basis and only where equivalent obligations apply, and the provider remains responsible for their compliance.
  6. Return or destruction. On request, the provider shall return or securely destroy Confidential Information within 5 business days and confirm in writing which archives were removed.
  7. Breach notification. The provider shall notify {{CLIENT}} within 72 hours of becoming aware of any unauthorised access to, or disclosure of, Confidential Information, with the facts known and the remediation taken.
  8. Audit. Once per engagement term, on reasonable notice, {{CLIENT}} may verify the controls described in Section 8, directly or through an adviser bound by confidentiality.
  9. No licence. Nothing in the engagement transfers intellectual property rights in Confidential Information; all rights remain with {{CLIENT}}.
  10. Term. These obligations survive completion of the engagement for three (3) years, and for any longer period required by applicable law or by the confidentiality of a live procurement.
What this statement does not claim. We do not hold an ISO/IEC 27001 or SOC 2 certification, and we make no claim of hosted disk-level encryption. The controls above are technical measures that can be inspected and tested by you, and we prefer to be judged on those rather than on certificates.
